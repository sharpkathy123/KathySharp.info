  • Maps

    You might want to print and bring some of these maps with you on the trip.

    • Driving Maps

      • Google Map: Driving Route
        • Point A is Denny's on Bell Road in Phoenix .
        • Point B is the McGuireville rest area on I-17.
        • Point C is Westside Lilo's Cafe in Seligman , which is near gas stations.
        • Point D is Grand Canyon Caverns , which has an inn, restaurant, and sometimes gas.
        • Point E is the south end of Tribal Road 18 .
        • Point F is our Thursday night rim campsite .
        • Point G is Hualapai Hilltop, which is the end of TR18, the place to drop off pack horse bags or get a helicopter ride, and the trailhead we start from on Friday morning.
      • Arizona Rest Areas - We'll probably use the McGuireville rest area

  • History

    • Freefall: Tragedy in the Grand Canyon - Backpacker Magazine article about the murder of a tourist in Havasu Canyon in 2006
    • My Days at Supai, by Gale Burak - from 1943
    • Grand Canyon Place Names - says of Cataract Canyon , "The bottom part of the canyon is the Havasupai reservation and known for a series of wonderful, blue, waterfalls. The village is called Supai. The creek is called Havasu Creek, but has also been called Coaini Creek."
    • Havasu Canyon Watershed Rapid Watershed Assessment Report June, 2010 - shows current mining (I think) on Figure 2-11 (other interesting stuff, too).
    • Hualapai Trail - has some interesting history for our trail .
    • Grand Canyon, Arizona (Page 2 of 3) Historic Human Occupation and Development Wikipedia - says, 'Mining interests also had a presence in the canyon in this period. Tunnels are a familiar sight in the campground area with the deepest of them around 300 feet (91 m). In Carbonate Canyon, adjacent to Havasu Falls, is the largest of the mines. Rails and timbers still can be found, and the mine descends three levels. Lead was mined here for the last time during WWII. Ranger Gale Burack, who worked at Grand Canyon, wrote of her experiences in the miners camp where the campground now resides in Arizona Highways Sept. 1984. Below Mooney Falls, the famous pipe "ladder" ascended to a vanadium deposit.'
