Accident Report for Near Drowning in April, 2005

This near-miss occurred in Cibecue Canyon, at the top of the slot just below the second waterfall. There were five people in the group, two men and two women, with one Apache Guide. The weather was good. The guide had taken the trail around the slot on canyon left while the others moved up through the slot. All five were fine with this plan.

The group approached the final challenge in the slot, about an eight foot waterfall. Most of the flow was coming down on the canyon left side. Below the waterfall, the slot was about five feet wide (just a little too wide to stem) and five feet deep. About 25 feet below the waterfall, there were some rocks blocking the slot that one could stand on. There was a move to be made to get above the waterfall. One person tried several times in several different ways to make the climb and fell off a couple of times. The last time he fell, he was pushed down to the bottom under the strong force of the waterfall current. His feet hit the bottom; he was compressed by the force of the water, and then surfaced quickly. He was tired at this point and another member of the party, who was a better climber, made the moves and got above the waterfall. A third member of the party started the climb. It is somewhat of an awkward move and eventually, this person fell too. Everyone fully expected this person to also surface quickly. She did not. Members of the group looked downstream to see if she was being swept away in the current. After about 10 seconds, no one could see her anywhere and at this point realized something was wrong. One person was in the water below the waterfall. He moved upstream to the waterfall and started to search the water. He swept the area with his feet and hands and came across her on the bottom about 10 feet below the climb. He indicated to the others that he had located her and then reached down to pull her up. He's not sure what part of her he grabbed and neither is she. She was coming up but then stopped rising. The person who was helping her could see her helmet about six inches under the surface. He pulled on her again and she surfaced. The estimate is that she was under the water for 35-45 seconds.

The group had their harnesses on and what happened is that some of her gear got caught in the rocks on the bottom after she fell. Obviously, she was trying to free herself. She never lost her breath and said that she knew she couldn't give up after she felt the other person find her. Her knee got twisted and she borrowed a trekking pole to assist her in the uneventful hike out of the canyon.

Author's personal thoughts on the incident:

I'm not sure what the lesson learned here is except a continued general caution that this sport is hazardous and there are risks. It never occurred to me that she might have her equipment caught. I either thought that she was being held under by the current or was just thinking she's not coming up and we need to do something quick. I don't know what I would have done if I couldn't have helped her get free. I had a rescue knife, but it sure would have been awkward to go under the water and start cutting things. I would think that the possibility of injuring either of us (especially her) with the knife would be medium to high. This was an accident with a good outcome. I realized how quickly adventure sports can change from having a great time, to having to deal with a life-threatening situation. I'm glad it ended in a relative quick way and didn't get much worse. I also wish I could have seen the looks on our faces. It was all kind of surreal at the time.

Another witness's comments:

A fourth member of the party was downstream during the incident, feeling completely helpless and not knowing what to do. She had gotten the impression that the water was much deeper at the waterfall than it really was, since people were staying under water for a while whenever they fell. She thought that, even if she tried to go upstream and look for the missing person, she wouldn't be able to reach deep enough and would be swept downstream before getting up to the waterfall area. She incorrectly thought that the missing person was being held deep under the waterfall by the force of the water.

Fourth party member's personal thoughts:

I'm so glad that someone in our party had more presence of mind than I had. I'm not sure how much I will do differently in the future, but I have already changed how I do things. Here are some things I found myself thinking while canyoneering since this incident.

  1. In places where people may go under water, have no slings or such hanging off a harness or anywhere on anyone. Note that a biner was NOT easily detachable during this incident. The gear that got caught was attached to the harness by a biner, but the victim couldn't get the biner off her harness due to the force of the water.
  2. Have more respect for flowing water. The situation looked difficult but not dangerous to me. I incorrectly thought that falling into the water would reduce the risk of trying to do the climb.
  3. Especially at bottlenecks in flowing water, immediately start thinking of options in case a rescue scenario occurs.
  4. Have a knife handy at all times. It probably wouldn't have helped in this situation, but I bought a rescue knife after this incident.
