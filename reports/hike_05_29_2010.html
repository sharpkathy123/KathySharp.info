Utah Canyoneering Trip May 29-June 6, 2010

Here's the list of who went on the trip:
  • Rogil Schroeter
  • Dave Cameron
  • Kathy Sharp
  • Tim Lange
  • Bill Fallon
  • Kim McClintic
  • Dave "Wilbur" McClintic

Introduction

Kathy: This year was less technical than usual. We had planned to do Neon Canyon, but the BLM office in Escalante told us that the Escalante River had been flowing much higher than normal since the previous weekend and so was probably not safe to cross where we would have to cross (on foot). This information was based on the flow of Boulder Creek(?), which flows into the river.

Tim: My first canyoneering trip with a new Canon HD camcorder. First impressions is that a wide-angle lens is necessary to get a reasonable perspective inside the canyon bottoms. 2nd discovery, my little laptop doesn't have the power to playback the HD clips; can't easily preview the captured video out in the field.

We never camped in the same place twice, although we did use a campsite used last year.

Day 1 – Drive to Utah

Kathy: We drove through Page and took Cottonwood Canyon Road. It was in pretty good shape considering the current controversy on who should maintain the road. We stopped in the BLM office in Cannonville, where we heard that the Smoky Mountain Road was passable with four wheel drive. Left Hand Collet, which connects to Hole in the Rock Road, was not passable at all, but work may be done to remedy that. The rangers told us of a place to camp before reaching Escalante; we turned off of Highway 12 at a sign that said, "North Creek Lakes."

Tim: There were a LOT of people camping along Cottonwood Canyon Rd and lots of cars in the parking lots at the trailheads. When at the Cannonville BLM office, they confirmed that they had been VERY busy over the last week. We topped off the water containers at a water spigot in the park across from the BLM office.

Campsite: There were not a lot of camping spots along the road where we eventually camped. There was an open area that looked like a gravel storage area or gravel "pit" that had a wide open area suitable for camping. We camped there our last night in the Escalante area.

Rogil: The camp was on Main Canyon Rd 5 miles W of Escalante

Day 2 – Red Breaks

Kathy: It had gotten so cold during the night that we had ice in our water in the morning. We headed for Hole in the Rock Road to do Red Breaks, which was a new canyon for all of us. I think we had three separate descriptions of how to do this canyon, and not one of them made sense or cleared up the questions about the other descriptions. So we probably didn’t do this trip the most efficient way, but we did have a good trip. We used Dave’s hook to provide a hand line down a spot that we think was described as a down climb with a fast slide, sometimes into a pool of water. There was no water. I think I didn’t eat enough calories that day because I was really, really tired at the end of the hike. Tim fed me and let me go to bed early.

Tim: We went up one fork, and when it began to open up, we crossed over to a larger fork to the East. We likely got out of the first, Up Canyon, fork too early and missed some of the narrows on the Down Canyon branch. The Up-Canyon section had some tight narrows. Due to the inconsistent route descriptions, we were uncertain we were in the right drop-in point for the Down-Canyon fork. We had quite a lot of discussion on whether to return the way we came, or continue into the Down-Canyon branch. We eventually decided on our original plan, dropped into the canyon and made our way back to the cars. This was shorter in time than returning the way we came and facing those tight narrows a 2nd time.

Campsite: on a bench S of the Harris Wash road, 1/4 mile from the Harris Wash trailhead. Large open area, nice campsite. We parked some cars here, due to parking lot being full at Harris Wash TH.

Rogil: We went up Big West Fork. It had a Very tight slot. The directions were confusing (& wrong), so we exited too early. But went over the ridge to Upper West Fork & dropped in right where the directions said to go, though we didn't recognize it at the time. First rap off a tree & last rap off Dave's hooks. Took us 9.5 hours

Day 3 – Egypt 1

Tim: Some guides stopped by and warned us that we were parked illegally (right in front of the dry-fall start of the route) and that they had set up anchors at all the rappels. We rapped in on a 60 foot rope.
I remember the canyon as being short and fun.

Campsite: We camped that night on a hill-top back a short ways towards Hole-In-The-Rock road. A larger campsite with yellow tub was at the end of the road, but it had a lot of cowpies. We chose a smaller campsite before this one and got all 4 vehicles to fit. We put up McClintic's sun shade between the cars, relaxed and planned our next conquest. The reality of the Keeper Pothole in Neon and not having up-to-date information about the condition led us to be conservative and put this off until we could get information in Escalante. We chose to do Egypt 2 in lieu of Neon.

Rogil: We left our first rap line & picked it up on the way back. Next rap, we fed the rope through a small hole, tied the middle to Dave & I rapped on the other end. Then I held the end coming through the small hole while everyone else rapped down. Then some hand-lines down and then 2 raps off deadmen that were set-up already. Several slides that were fun. Getting out, Bill climbed the steep slick rock & made us a hand line to use. Stay near the canyon on the walk back to the car for the shortest hike. It took us 3 hours. We could have run it twice.

Day 4 – Egypt 2

Kathy: Our longest rope was 200 feet, so we walked to the gully entrance and then rappelled into the canyon. We left our vehicles where we planned to come out of the canyon, which is where we at first thought the canyon entry was.

Tim: One entry option was a 250' rap, we didn't have enough rope and therefore walked in using a gully to the East. We eventually rapped 70' to get into the main canyon. We overshot our exit point and turned around at a sheltered area with large cottonwood trees that had shed their seeds; it looked like a blanket of white snow several inches thick. After backtracking a short distance, we climbed out of the canyon bottom and took a direct route up through a break in the bench; Bill led a short steep section and then gave us a hand-line assist. It was quite steep and the sandstone wasn't real solid. The route description suggested going around the bench to the West (less direct). There had been some foot traffic where we went up. We did notice fewer footprints in the stream bed at this exit point, suggesting that an earlier exit point is being used, perhaps by the guides.

This was very similar in character to Egypt 1, but longer in length.

Campsite: We camped in an open area along the road to Coyote Gulch, South side of the road. It was windy and we wanted some shelter and picked a relative low spot along the road. This worked out well, still enough breeze to keep the bugs at bay.

Rogil: Directions said there were 3 raps, so we kept hiking looking for the 3rd rap. Glad we over-shot our exit as the confluence was worth the hike. Exit was just up canyon from a drainage on the right. Took us 5 hours.

Day 5 – Coyote Gulch

Kathy: I am always happy to do Coyote Gulch. I had forgotten that the "sand trap" road was doable with four wheel drive. Coyote Gulch is a gorgeous canyon, and being able to hike in the water is really refreshing on a hot day. We hike in at Crack in the Wall and go down the big sand dune, and we exit at Jacob Hamblin Arch, which is a steep, unprotected climb on slick rock. Why anyone would want to go down that (scarier than going up) and up the sand dune, I don’t know. We took the quick side trip to the Escalante River and found that it was indeed flowing higher than we remembered. Coyote Gulch, Stevens Arch, and Coyote Natural Bridge were as beautiful as ever. We ran into lots of hikers and found camps, occupied and not, all along the creek. A place that some of us remembered standing under a waterfall was dry—the water course had moved several feet away. (A nice new toilet has been installed at Jacob Hamblin Arch.) We filled our water containers at the spring at Jacob Hamblin Arch before heading out of the canyon. Exiting at Jacob Hamblin Arch can be a bit sketchy, but we were all rock climbers, so everyone did fine. Kim and Wilbur started their drive back to Phoenix that afternoon.

Tim: A new section of rock-fall was above the relocated waterfall Kathy mentions. I get a little weary after this much time on my feet. A fun canyon to wander through, very expansive vistas.

Campsite: We visited the Batty Caves (West of HITR road) found lots of little gnats, then picked a campsite on the way back toward HITR road. This was the buggiest site all week, lots of bites. Kathy and I waited until the sun went down and the bugs were less active, then did our cooking. In the morning, we went to the TH to do Spooky and Peekaboo and didn't see any obvious campsites along this section of the road. Camping at the TH is no longer permitted.

Rogil: Was a great day. The moki steps seemed pretty easy this time. We took 9 hours, partly due to thinking the road would be impassable, so didn't run a shuttle.

Day 6 – Spooky and Peekaboo Gulches

Kathy: Bill and Dave had never been to these canyons, which everyone who visits Escalante should see. In the past, we had only visited the lower canyons, but this trip we visited the middle narrows of Spooky. We’re thinking we’ll have to come back and do some variation of Kelsey’s trip from a road to the north down one gulch and up the other—a long day, but we'll get to see some more new narrows.

Tim: I enjoyed Spooky, some interesting tight areas. Visiting the middle narrows was worth the slog through the sand.

Campsite: MP 70 at a cattle grate, near Spencer Flats, in a gully leading to the shelf overlooking a large sandstone basin. The phallic-like rock is a landmark to the South. We parked the cars, cooked dinner then took the chairs and enjoyed the open vistas and sunset. A few bugs, not bad.

Rogil: I was thinking next time to do a lollypop hike from the North: down Spooky leaving a rope on the rappel, up Peek-a-boo, across to Spooky above the narrows & return up Spooky. Going down-stream in the tight parts of lower Spooky shouldn't be as bad as going up them. We spent 7 hours playing here.

Day 7 – Calf Creek

Kathy: Bill left to drive home in the morning. Last year we rappelled down both the upper and lower falls at Calf Creek, but this year we decided to just take an easy day to hike in the water from the upper falls to the top of the lower falls and back. This is a beautiful hike. Tim took waypoints at possible backpacking campsites.

Tim: We explored above the upper falls but it was choked with vegetation and we soon turned around. There are some nice pools above the upper falls. I originally thought this would be a recovery day, but, finished tired due to all the walking in water, especially upstream against the flow. Very glad I wore the 5.10 Canyoneer shoes, the extra grip was noticeable.

Campsite: the gravel pit area a few miles West of Escalante, a short distance on gravel road South of the highway.

Rogil: Trailhead is at MP 80.8. The road was renumbered, so Kelsey's book is wrong. We took our time on this one so that we'd return to the cars after the worse of the heat: 9 hours. Fortunately we got thin clouds and a breeze, so the hike up wasn't bad.

Day 8 – Keyhole

Kathy: Rogil’s toe had been acting up the previous day, so we decided to go tubing at Zion. However, we found that the tubing place in Springdale that we had gone to before had closed up shop, and the Zion Adventure Company had tubes out but was not doing trips that day. (They won’t be tubing till the river level goes down, or were they just trying out tubing to decide whether they want that business?) We had our pizza leftovers (from the previous night) at a Springdale park and got permits to do a quick trip through Keyhole Canyon in Zion, a very short, very nice little canyon. Tim suggested taking photos of the trip description and map from my guide book, since we didn't want to carry the book but I always have my waterproof pocket camera (a gift from Tim). Great idea! We got a head start on our drive home, picked up delicious sandwiches at the Three Bears in Kanab, and camped in the cool pines near Jacob Lake.

Tim: The Three Bears is also an ice cream shop; it is on the way out of Kanab, on the West side of the road. Homemade bread used in the sandwiches and for sale.

Campsite: less than 10 miles before Jacob Lake.

Rogil: This was a great canyon. I would have loved to do it twice. Much warmer than last time. Took us less than 2 hours, but some of that time was spent waiting for an exceptionally slow group in front of us.

Day 9 – Drive to Phoenix

Kathy: We had camped west of Jacob Lake, so we went into Jacob Lake for breakfast.

Conclusion


Kathy: Beautiful canyons, not a drop of rain, great friends to enjoy it all with—what more could we ask for?!
