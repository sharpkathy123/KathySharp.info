Utah Canyoneering Trip May 26-June 3, 2012

Dave Cameron, Julie Dehlin, Bill Fallon, Tim Lange, Mark Lucas, Troy Lucas, Rogil Schroeter, and Kathy Sharp went on their annual pilgrimage to visit Utah canyons this year.

We spent a week in the Robbers Roost area. We had brought drybags and wetsuits to keep our options open, but all the canyons we did this year were either completely dry or only had mud or a bit of shallow water. The area must have been pretty dry for quite some time.

Here’s our trip report, along with links to Kathy's trip photos.

Day 1, Saturday May 26, 2012, Drive to Utah

We camped the first three nights just off the Hans Flat Road about a mile north of the Ekker Ranch Road turnoff. We could have also camped a little further down our side road, at the West Fork of Bluejohn trailhead.

We spent another night south of there on Hans Flat Road. Gnats were a little pesky at both campsites.

Hans Flat Ranger Station (further south) has a pit toilet and 7.5 minute maps you can buy.

Day 2, Sunday, May 27, 2012, Tidwell Canyon

This was our first trip through Tidwell Canyon. We liked it and plan to revisit it.

The directions say there’s a sandy track to the trailhead but we had no problem driving to the trailhead. A big, wooden flywheel and other old debris were at the trailhead, which is an old drill-hole site. We went cross-country to get in and out of the canyon. Follow the wash to access the top of the canyon and avoid disturbing cryptobiotic soil. We did an in-and-out trip, and it took us 7 hours.

We did two rappels, which we ascended on our return. A few people went down to look at the third rap; it was long and not something we wanted to ascend. The first rap was from a piton and was in a narrow slot. Rapping from a natural anchor would have been super awkward but the piton made all the difference since it was out where the slot opened up. We used Kathy’s ascenders for each person to ascend, then a hand-line to bring them across into the slot.

To ascend the second rap, we ran a rope down to the climber, through his harness, and back to the top. Someone pulled on the loose end of the rope to assist while the climber monkey-manned up. It was very efficient.

Day 3, Monday, May 28, 2012, Bluejohn Canyon and Horseshoe Canyon

We did a long, hot slog (12 miles total?) through lots of soft sand but saw a nice, short section of narrows that none of us had seen before, even though we’d done other parts of Bluejohn Canyon. We exited by doing a one-way trip through great Horseshoe Canyon rock art. Some of us ran out of water before reaching the end.

Saving time by hiking into Bluejohn Canyon via the Main Exit route saved us no time. The West Fork option is better. The trip took us 9 hours from car to car. Dave wasn’t feeling the greatest and stayed in camp that day.

We were beat after this trip, so this version of Bluejohn is a been-there-done-that trip.

(Read more about Horseshoe Canyon rock art here: http://www.nps.gov/cany/planyourvisit/upload/HorseshoeBook.pdf)

Day 4, Tuesday, May 29, 2012, West Fork of White Roost Canyon

Julio left for Phoenix and the rest of us headed for something less taxing than the previous day’s trip. The West Fork of White Roost was a repeat for some of us and took us 5 hours this time.

We accidentally headed for the main fork entry coordinates. Fortunately, Mark questioned our heading. Also, only one of us realized when we were at the confluence with White Roost Canyon, after emerging from a tight, left-leaning slot.

We used ourselves as meat anchors at the first drop. The canyon had only one puddle, which was shin deep, and we could stem over it.

Once out of the canyon, remember that it’s faster and easier to head for the road rather than for the cars.

Day 5, Wednesday, May 30, 2012, Robbers Roost Canyon North Fork

This was our first time in this canyon, and we liked it, so we’ll repeat this one. The canyon had beautiful, soaring walls. It took us 7.5 hours.

We rapped down the third dropoff and then climbed back up it to a different exit. Mark checked out the Crack Exit and did the first two chimneys and said it looked okay, so we may do that next time. We enjoyed climbing up the dry waterfall rather than ascending it.

We had Bill’s and Dave’s cars at the entrance trailhead for the canyon, at the end of a sometimes impassable sandy track that we had no problem driving. We hiked out and up to Mark’s and Kathy’s vehicles. The original plan was to hike back to the two cars at the canyon trailhead. However, there was a road on Mark’s updated phone topo map that was not on the old USGS printed topo. We walked this road back to the sandy track and then at that point walked up to our vehicles parked at the fence at the top of the sandy track.

Day 6, Thursday, May 31, 2012, High Spur

This canyon is also known as Spur Canyon Northeast Fork.

We had planned to do Not Mindbender this day, but we found that the Robbers Roost Spring Road was being overtaken by a sand dune. A ranger at Hans Flat Ranger Station said that the roads get graded in March and October, so the dune will probably only get bigger this summer.

The drive to the High Spur trailhead is long. Also, next time we’ll park the shuttle vehicle(s) exactly at the coordinates we had, at the top of the hill. Kathy was glad that Tim did the shuttle driving since the shuttle road was quite rough in a couple places.

Rogil called this canyon a “string of pearls.” The canyon alternated between open spots and many very photogenic narrows. We really enjoyed the beauty. A knee-deep stretch of pancake batter mud was unexpected. (Did anyone get a photo of Troy’s face?) We’d like to repeat this canyon. It took us 6.5 hours.

Mark and Troy split off to head toward home and do Fry Canyon the next day.

Day 7, Friday, June 1, 2012, Larry Canyon

We did this canyon last year and, after this year’s trip, Tim decided that he has done this canyon enough times.

The canyon had less water this year. There was only one section of water; it was at the bottom of one of the rappels, and it was only calf deep.

This canyon has some tight narrows, but it’s wide enough that Dave, who doesn’t like the real tight squeezes, enjoyed the canyon.

We took nine hours to do this canyon from car to car. We had minimal flesh loss compared to last year. We were beat after this trip.

Day 7, Friday, June 1, 2012, Fry Canyon: Troy and Mark

We skipped doing the actual canyon because we had already done it before, and we hiked along the right side of the canyon along the rim to rap down into an old Indian ruin. Rapping in directly above the ruin is at least a 125 foot rap, easily more. From here, hike back up canyon along the rim about 100 yards. Using a long sling, we made an anchor around a boulder and threw it down about 60 feet to a shelf that was easy to downclimb. There are no bolts around here. From here, you can use at least a 100 foot rope to reach the shelf the ruin is on. After rapping down, walk along the shelf about 100 yards, and the ruin is on the right. It is a decent ruin still in good shape, but is picked clean. We found a pottery shard and an old corn cob which we left. Below the ruin about 25 feet is a set of 2 bolts. This is one of the only ways down this shelf to the bottom of the canyon. It is about a 55 foot rap all the way down. From here, we hiked to a side canyon on the right, took the canyon for a little ways, and found a trail to hike out on.

We drove home that day.

Day 8, Saturday, June 2, 2012, Dinosaur Quarry and Robotics Competition

Bill left in the morning, and we drove to the Red Rock Steak House/Campground in Hanksville for showers and directions to a dinosaur quarry. We drove west out of Hanksville and north on Cow Dung Road for 7 to 8 miles or so to the signed quarry. Cow Dung Road is interesting and otherworldly.

The Hanksville-Burpee Dinosaur Quarry was not open for tours yet, but they welcomed us and gave us a very interesting tour anyway. The site is only worked a couple months per year, and the free tours are usually in early June.

We stopped along Cow Dung Road to watch some of the 2012 University Rover Challenge on our way back from the quarry. We also stopped to take photos of the Mars Habitat but did not check to see if anyone was there for a tour. (See http://www.marssociety.org for more about the robotics challenge and the Mars Desert Research Station.)

We camped at Muley Point, for which there is a sign at the top of the Moqui Dugway. We had a nice breeze, great temperature, magnificent views except for the haze (from a New Mexico fire maybe?), a beautiful sunset, and not many gnats.

Day 9, Sunday, June 3, 2012, Drive to Phoenix

We stopped at Goosenecks State Park and then had a delicious breakfast at the restaurant at the bridge over the San Juan River in Mexican Hat. We also stopped at the Cameron Trading Post for ice cream, fudge, and garden photos on the way home.
