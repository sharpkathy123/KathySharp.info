Salome Creek - The Jug

The Jug in Salome Creek is usually our first canyoneering trip of the year and is usually in early May. On Saturday, the AMC people go through. On Sunday the VOA volunteers go through the canyon.

If you are interested in the Saturday trip, please contact Rogil and Kathy.

Each trip is limited to 15 people (Salome Wilderness rules). We will be doing trips on Saturday and Sunday, combining our group with VOA volunteers, who may not have rappelling experience. On Saturday, the VOA volunteers will be working on the trail we will use to exit Salome Creek.

From the trailhead, we will hike to the creek (about an hour), meet up with the VOA participants, and then swim, wade, climb, slide, and rappel down the creek. Plan to be in cold water and shade a long time. Then we'll hike back to the cars.

The VOA volunteers, possibly including Rogil and Kathy, will be camping Saturday night at a group site reserved for VOA volunteers.

Supplies

Here is the required supplies list for our group:

  • drybag (zip lock baggies and trash bags do NOT work for this trip)
  • life vest
  • harness or 20-foot sling
  • 2 locking carabiners
  • rappel device
  • prusiks
  • personal anchor

(VOA volunteers need only bring the drybag and life vest. We will supply what they need of the other above equipment.)

And here is a list of optional but highly recommended supplies for everyone:

  • helmet for protection from slips, falls, etc.
  • wetsuit (Even when the air temperature is hot, we often end up with people not wearing wetsuits getting close to hypothermia.)
  • shoes with excellent traction when wet (While in the creek, you will be wearing these for hiking, wading, and swimming.)
  • pack to protect your drybag from getting abrasion holes (You can put grommet holes in the bottom of the pack to let water drain out so that water doesn't make your pack weigh a ton.)
  • sacrificial shorts to protect the wetsuit from damage somewhat

We sometimes have a couple helmets, PFDs, and wetsuits you can borrow, but only if you let us know you need one.

Of course, you'll need hiking supplies, such as:

  • pack
  • hat
  • 2 liters or 1/2 gallon of water
  • lunch (in your drybag)
  • thick socks (so you don't feel every pebble that washes into your shoes while in the water)
  • sun block
  • walking stick
  • headlamp
  • toilet paper and baggy for used stuff (no opportunity to use this while in the canyon, though)
You can wear cotton for the dry hiking on this trip, but do NOT wear cotton clothing in the creek, since you cannot warm up while wearing wet cotton and it takes too long to dry. Most of us wear quick-dry clothes like CoolMax, nylon, and polypropylene for canyoneering. For this trip, you can bring both hiking and wading shoes if you want. (We can hike to close to the creek, cache some stuff, then do the creek, hike back to the cache, and then hike back to the cars.)

A waterproof camera would be nice for in the canyon.

Motorola radios are handy for the caravan to the trailhead.

Go to the checklists link for our full hiking and canyoneering supply checklists.

Weather

A rain forecast anywhere that drains into Salome Creek--and flash flood danger--would cancel this trip, of course.

We will have to cross Tonto Creek to get to the trailhead. Check Real-Time Data for Arizona Streamflow.

We will be meeting on Saturday morning in the parking lot near the IHOP near Pima and Shea, which is now near the eastern portion of Loop 101. Here are directions to the meeting place.
  • Get off Loop-101 at Shea.
  • Go east 1 block to light at 90 St.
  • Turn south (right) and go about 1/4 mile.

Trailhead

Be sure to let the organizers know if you will not be meeting us at the IHOP!

From the meeting place, we'll probably take Shea east to 87, 87 north to 188, and 188 southeast to Punkin Center. Here are directions from there to the trailhead from the book Canyoneering Arizona, by Tyler Williams.

Take highway 188 south from Punkin Center about 8 miles. Near milepost 255, take road #60 (also known as A Cross Road) to the east for 10.1 miles. Park at "The Jug" trailhead.

Unless the trailhead sign has changed, it doesn't say "The Jug," but there aren't lots of trailhead signs in the area to confuse us.

The above route may be flooded. If so:

  • We'll cross at Bar X Road between MPs 259 and 258. The road winds through some residential areas (on Cline Blvd.?) for several miles before joining up with A Cross Road.
  • OR we'll go from Globe to SR-88/188 to SR-288. E to FR-60 (12 miles). Turn Left to TH (12 miles).

New to This?

This trip is absolutely great fun if you're ready for it! But, unfortunately, we discovered in the past that these kinds of trips can be absolute torture for someone who's not ready, and the resulting debacle can ruin the trip for everyone.

If you're new to this kind of trip, here are some things to consider.

  • The hike up and out of the canyon is significant and may be hot, so you'll need to have done some uphill hike training in the heat.
  • Some unroped rock climbing may be required. Usually the multitudes of little down climbs are not too difficult, and everyone spots each other, but an inexperienced climber or someone too afraid of heights might have no fun.
  • Depending on the water level, to get to the rappel station, you may be required to walk across a wet, narrow, water polished, slippery ledge that slopes toward a big drop. Occasionally, the water is really low and we can bypass that ledge by rappelling right in the waterfall.
  • On a past trip to a different canyon, we had one person with us who had never rappelled, but he promised he'd do it. However, he could not make himself step off the edge of the cliff. In that canyon, there was another way down. At Salome the only option to rappelling is walking the narrow ledge and jumping off a high cliff into the water. By the time you're at the Salome rappel station, it's very unlikely that you can go back the way you came, unless you can climb up smooth, overhung walls.
  • People in our canyoneering group have seen serious injuries from slipping while simply walking on deceptively slimy surfaces, a fall requiring stitches from stepping off a rock and a shoe catching on a bush, and a near drowning from falling underwater and getting stuck.
  • Canyoneering is dangerous. As always, you participate at your own risk.
