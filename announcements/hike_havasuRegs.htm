Havasu Campgrounds Regulations

Terms and Conditions of Your Entrance to the Havasupai Reservation

(from May 2006)

  • Read, understand, and agree to abide by the laws of the Havasupai Tribe and the rules and regulations contained in the General Information Guide.
  • Camp only in designated campground sites.
  • Pack out all trash.
  • Will not have a campfire.
  • Refrain from diving or attempting to climb rocks, waterfalls, cliffs or canyon walls.
  • Will not possess or use alcohol or any illegal drugs within the boundaries of the Havasupai Reservation.
  • Violations may result in fines, exclusion from the Havasupai Reservation, seizure of property and possible prosecution under the jurisdiction of the Havasupai Tribal Court.
  • Refunds will not be given in the event of such violations or if an emergency situation or natural disaster evacuation necessitates early departure.
  • The Havasupai Tribal Council, Tribal Enterprises, tribal employees and individual tribal members shall not be held responsible or liable for damage, injury, or loss to person or property that may be the result of accidents, emergency conditions or any other occurrence during the course of our stay.

GENERAL INFORMATION GUIDE

Please abide by the following entrance rules and regulations. We advise that you heed the safety recommendations, failure to do so could result in loss of personal property, serious injury, illness, or even death.

The trail to Supai is 8 miles from Hualapai Hilltop. The campground is 2 miles and waterfalls are between 1 ½ - 2 ½ miles beyond the village. There is a small cafe and store in Supai. A map is available at the Tourist Office. An ADVANCE RESERVATION for lodging, camping and horses is required. All visitors are required to pay entrance fees and obtain permits payable upon arrival in the village at the Tourist Office or Lodge. Permit tags issued should be attached to daypacks or otherwise be readily available for inspection during your stay.

Pack or saddle horse arrangements must be made 24 hours in advance. Penalties for late arrival, no shows or cancellations may apply. We ask that you utilize the tribal owned services as your first choice for packing supplies and riding in/out of the canyon. Helicopter services are privately owned and operated. All helicopter transportation arrangements must be made directly with the helicopter service personnel. When traveling on Indian Highway 169 be especially watchful for wildlife and free roaming livestock crossing the road. There is no gas or food services at Hualapai Hilltop.

Plan to hike in the coolest part of the day, when the canyon is shady. Hiking at night is not recommended; if you do so, take a flashlight and extra batteries. In hot sunny weather, take frequent rest stops in whatever shade you can find to allow your body temperature to cool down. If necessary, wait until the evening shade to continue. A sun hat, sunscreen, extra socks, and appropriate attire for the weather conditions are recommended. Wear comfortable well-fitting hiking shoes with good ankle support. We recommend rubber-sole shoes for use in the water.

Carry a minimum of 1 GALLON OF WATER PER PERSON ON THE HIKE. NO WATER IS AVAILABLE ALONG THE TRAIL. IF YOU DO NOT HAVE ADEQUATEWATER DO NOT START DOWN THE TRAIL! Watch for SIGNS OF DEHYDRATION in yourself and group members which include: headache, dizziness, moodiness and fatigue. Dangerous or late signs of dehydration are: fast heartbeat, fast shallow breathing, confusion and disorientation. Drinking water is available from the water faucet at the Tourist Office, Lodge and Store in Supai. Filter water taken from the creek or springs before drinking or using for cooking.

KEEP YOUR GROUP TOGETHER AT ALL TIMES and do not hike alone. It is especially important that you do not allow children to go ahead of the hiking group or tag behind. Hike only on prescribed trails and do not take side canyons or explore old mine shafts or caves as they are extremely hazardous. Camping is allowed ONLY in designated campground sites which are available on a first-come-first-serve basis. CAMPFIRES ARE STICTLY PROHIBITED!

HORSES have the right-of-way on the trail. Yield to all horses and riders. Do not frighten the horses with sudden movements. Watch out for and respect snakes and scorpions. Shake out your shoes before putting them on. Regard unstable ground warning signs.

Although floods can happen at any time in the year, the monsoon season extends from June to September. Watch for dark storm clouds but be aware that flash floods can occur without any warning and without any sign of rain where you are hiking. If you hear floodwaters approaching or if you are caught in a rainstorm, climb or move your campsite to high ground immediately and wait until it clears up. DO NOT ENTER NARROW PARTS OF THE CANYONWHEN IT IS RAINING OR FLOODING!

There are NO PUBLIC HEALTH CARE FACITILIES in the village and it could take many hours to get medical treatment or to be transported out of the canyon. Emergency transportation will be very costly for you and may not be available at night or in certain weather conditions. Bring along minor first aid supplies such as band aides, moleskin, first ointment, etc.

The descent below Mooney Falls is extremely treacherous and is not recommended for children or adults with physical limitations. The trails and areas beyond Mooney Falls are strictly for DAY-USE ONLY. Overnight camping requires advance Tribal Council permission.

Children must be accompanied by an adult at all times. Safeguard your personal property and keep money or valuables with you at all times. Be certain all your belongings are clearly marked or tagged with your name, address and telephone number. Rangers are stationed at the Campground Ranger Station during tourist season. The security of your automobile and property left at Hualapai Hilltop can not be guaranteed as year-round 24-hour security or services are not provided.

Public telephones are not always accessible or in working order at Hualapai Hilltop or in Supai. Emergencies may be reported to the BIA Law Enforcement Agency (928) 448-2891 or in person at the BIA detention facility in Supai.

Filming or photography for commercial purposes is STRICTLY PROHIBITED and requires prior approval by the Havasupai Tribal Council. Please do not take pictures of our children without having first obtained permission from their parent to do so. Public nudity is not allowed. The residential vicinity and trails are restricted access areas. Thank you for respecting the privacy of Tribal members.

***** Your voluntary entrance into the Havasupai Reservation boundaries implies that you agree to comply with the laws and regulations of the Havasupai Tribe; and that you understand violations may result in fines, exclusion from the Reservation, seizure of property and possible prosecution under the jurisdiction of the Havasupai Tribal Court. The Havasupai Tribal Council, Tribal Enterprises, tribal employees and individual tribal members shall not be held responsible or liable for damage, injury, or loss to person or property that may be the result of accidents, emergency conditions or any other occurrence during the course of your stay. *****

  • PAY ENTRANCE FEES UPON ARRIVAL
  • Do not BRING OR USE ALCOHOL OR ILLEGAL DRUGS
  • PACK OUT ALL OF YOUR TRASH
  • Do not DIVE FROM ROCKS OR WATERFALLS
  • CAMP ONLY IN DESIGNATED CAMPSITES
  • Do not ATTEMPT TO CLIMB CLIFFS OR CANYON WALLS

We hope you enjoy your visit and will return again soon!
